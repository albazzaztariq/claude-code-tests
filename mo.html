The Competitor

Egypt's Greatest on the Mat

Mohamed Abdelfatah — known throughout the wrestling world as "Bogy" — spent nearly two decades as one of the most feared Greco-Roman wrestlers on the planet. Competing primarily at 84 kilograms for Egypt, he combined raw physical dominance with technical precision, locking and lifting opponents in a style that was equal parts brutal and beautiful.

His resume speaks for itself: a Junior World silver, two World Cup titles, participation in three Olympic Games, and close to two dozen international tournament victories across a career that spanned the late 1990s through 2012. He is one of only two Egyptians in history to win a Wrestling World Championship.

After the 2004 Athens Olympics, US Greco-Roman legend Steve Fraser invited Abdelfatah into the Special Foreign Athletes Program at the Olympic Training Center in Colorado Springs. He arrived at 26, and never really left. "Since the first time I came here, I never felt like I was foreign," he says. "We were like a family. I never felt like an outsider."

The Moment That Defined a Career

At the 2006 World Championships, Abdelfatah stood across the mat from Nazmi Avluca — the reigning World Champion from Turkey, one of the most celebrated Greco-Roman wrestlers of his era. Turkey had a massive crowd behind their man. Egypt had almost none. But the entire US contingent — his adopted wrestling family — roared for him.

He won. The gold medal made him only the second Egyptian in history to claim a Wrestling World Championship. Back home, it meant celebrity. Here at the OTC, it meant something different. "My American friends and training partners, we are like a family. This place is just always in my heart."

"The US fans were all united and cheering, yelling 'Go Mohamed, go!' That's my family."

— Mohamed Abdelfatah, on the 2006 World Championship final

The Coach

Building the Next Generation

When his competitive days wound down after London 2012, Abdelfatah channeled everything he had learned on the mat into coaching. By 2017 he was the head coach of the US Greco-Roman program at the Olympic Training Center — the most prominent coaching post in American wrestling.

His approach is intensely personal. He builds individual training plans, tailors technique work to each athlete's body and strengths, and earns trust the way he always earned respect — by being in the room, working hard, and knowing the sport better than almost anyone alive.

Among the athletes he has coached and worked with at the highest level:

Kyle Snyder Trent Hidlay G'Angelo Hancock Kamal Bey Robby Smith Patrick Martinez RaVaughn Perkins Andy Bisek & many others

"He is a real coach. He's got a heart for helping these guys. Mo has no ego in the game — he's very humble."

— Matt Lindland, US Greco-Roman National Team Head Coach
